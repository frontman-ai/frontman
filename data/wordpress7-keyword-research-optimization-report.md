## Winning Patterns Discovered

### Pattern 1: "We Did the Work" Authority
Both winning posts lead with evidence of actual testing/building. Not "here's what the docs say" but "here's what we found." This pattern scored 5-8 points higher than desk-research framing across all judges, especially the skeptical founder (+10 avg).

### Pattern 2: Specific Numbers Build Trust
"50+ plugins", "23 broke immediately", "11 broke silently" — concrete numbers consistently outscored vague quantifiers ("many", "several", "most"). The CMO and skeptical founder weighted these highest.

### Pattern 3: Cycle Framing for CTAs
The winning CTAs frame WordPress maintenance as a recurring cycle (WP7 → WP8 → WP9), not a one-time event. This makes the "escape to Frontman" pitch feel like a structural argument rather than an opportunistic sales push.

### Pattern 4: Gap-Calling for SEO Authority
"Zero tutorials exist", "nobody else covers this", "the guide that should have shipped with core" — calling out content gaps scored well with the CRO (establishes first-mover framing) and copywriter (differentiation).

---

## Risk Flags

1. **Post A title claims "50+" tests** — the actual blog post must deliver on this. If the real testing is thinner, use the runner-up title (84.6) which doesn't commit to a number.

2. **Post C hook names Claude specifically** — consider whether naming a competitor's product in the first paragraph is strategically wise. The runner-up hook (84.8) is product-agnostic: "There are exactly zero independent tutorials..."

3. **Both CTAs are assertive about WordPress being legacy** — this could alienate WordPress-loyal readers. The posts need to earn this positioning with the preceding content, not just assert it in the CTA.
