Guy Leshno, Marketing Lead at [AutonomyAI](https://autonomyai.io/), needed to redesign the company's WordPress marketing site. He had never worked with WordPress before.

Guy reported that AutonomyAI received agency quotes in the several-thousand-dollar range. He expected an agency-led redesign and review cycle to take months. AutonomyAI also considered leaving WordPress for an AI website builder such as Lovable or Base44.

Using Frontman with the existing Elementor page-builder site, Guy changed the visual theme, redesigned almost every core marketing page, added use-case pages, worked on about five articles, and launched the new version himself. He completed the project part-time in roughly two weeks, including stakeholder review.

**Disclosure and evidence status:** Frontman produced this customer case study about its own product. The author, Danni Friedland, is a Frontman co-founder. Unless linked to a public source, project scope, duration, agency quotes, and prior WordPress experience are Guy's account. Public sources checked on August 27, 2026 confirm [Guy's role at AutonomyAI](https://autonomyai.io/human-intelligence/), the current WordPress site, and Frontman's documented Elementor workflow. This was not a controlled benchmark or conversion study.

## The old site no longer matched the product

[AutonomyAI](https://autonomyai.io/) builds Fei Studio, an AI delivery system that helps product managers and designers turn product decisions into code that engineers can review and merge. As the company sharpened that positioning, its marketing site needed more than new hero copy.

The homepage had to explain a different product story. New role and use-case pages had to support it. Existing pages and articles had to align with the same message and visual direction.

Guy knew what the site needed to communicate. He did not know how to build it in WordPress.

## AutonomyAI considered an agency and a platform migration

The conventional option was to hire a WordPress agency. Guy reported that AutonomyAI received quotes in the several-thousand-dollar range. He expected the redesign, feedback cycle, and launch to take months.

The team also considered moving the site to Lovable, Base44, or another AI-first builder.

WordPress already supplied the content management system (CMS), plugins, and publishing workflow. Rebuilding elsewhere meant migrating that setup and depending on another provider. Once Frontman gave Guy a visual AI editor inside WordPress, moving no longer offered AutonomyAI a clear advantage.

The site could stay where it was. Guy could change it himself.

## Guy worked from the page, not the WordPress implementation

Guy's workflow stayed close to the visible site:

1. Install and activate the Frontman WordPress plugin.
2. Open the site through Frontman's live preview.
3. Select the page element or section that needs to change.
4. Describe the intended result in plain language.
5. Review the visible result and request another pass when needed.
6. Share the updated page with stakeholders and incorporate feedback.
7. Publish the accepted version.

Frontman executed supported WordPress and Elementor changes inside that loop. Guy selected targets, described the intended result, reviewed each visible change, and decided what to publish. He did not have to learn how to implement every change in Elementor or hire someone to translate his requests into page-builder work.

Some changes needed several prompt iterations. Stakeholders reviewed the messaging and design, and Guy decided when each result was ready to publish.

This case study does not expose Guy's exact prompts or a task-by-task change log. For a separately documented example, [watch one complete staging-safe WordPress task](/blog/best-wordpress-ai-plugins-2026/#frontman-workflow-demo) from request through reviewed result.

## What the result shows

Guy reported completing and launching the redesign in roughly two weeks of part-time work. Production task metadata records 10 active days in the confirmed 15-day window and a conservative 24.0 hours of session activity. He also reported agency quotes in the several-thousand-dollar range and expected that route to take months. The project did not record a payroll-grade work-hour total or final page inventory.

Guy still made the marketing decisions. He owned the positioning, content, visual judgment, stakeholder review, and final publication. Frontman executed supported WordPress and Elementor changes from his requests.

One customer result cannot promise the same timeline for every theme or plugin setup. It does show what changed for Guy: he started with no WordPress experience and finished with a redesigned marketing site that he launched himself.

AutonomyAI kept its existing CMS, plugins, and publishing workflow without pursuing the agency route or moving to another builder.

## What this result does not prove

This was one customer project, not a controlled comparison between Frontman, an agency, and an AI website builder. Frontman did not review redacted agency proposals, measure an agency implementation, or calculate cost savings from recorded work hours.

The project also did not measure conversion lift, search traffic, accessibility, page speed, or post-launch defect rate. The evidence supports a narrower conclusion: Guy reported completing a broad Elementor redesign himself, the redesigned site is live, and public product documentation supports the workflow he described.

Theme, plugin, hosting, and custom-Elementor setups vary. A result on AutonomyAI's site does not establish the same timeline for another WordPress stack.
